# axol, explained without the jargon

**What it is.** axol is a short-video app, the kind you scroll with your thumb like TikTok, YouTube
Shorts or Instagram Reels. The difference is who the feed works for. Every other feed is built by a
company to keep you on *their* platform and show you *their* ads. axol has no platform to protect.
It pulls short videos from all three of those services and shows you only the ones chosen for you.

**How it gets to know you.** When you sign up, you don't tick boxes. You just write a few sentences,
the way you'd tell a friend: "I'm into skateboarding and jazz piano, I'm trying to get better at
cooking, I love oddly satisfying videos, and I never want to see politics." axol reads that the way a
person would, works out what you actually care about, and goes looking for videos on all three
platforms at once.

**How it gets better.** This is the part that matters. axol doesn't trust what you *say* you like.
It watches what you *do*. Every time a video plays, it quietly measures how much of it you watched.
Did you skip after two seconds? Did you watch to the end? Did you let it loop? Did you tap the heart?
Each of those nudges your profile a little, and the next videos reflect it. You can literally see it
happen: after a clip, a small note appears saying something like "Learned: more jazz piano."

Every dozen videos, axol steps back and rewrites its description of your taste in plain English,
notices patterns you didn't spell out, and sends out new searches based on what's really holding your
attention. It tells you what it figured out. You can open your profile at any time, see every interest
it's tracking, and turn any of them up, down, or off.

**Why it feels good to use.** Most of the feed is reliably the stuff you love. But every batch also
contains one deliberate surprise, a video from just outside your usual interests, to see if it lands.
If it does, your world gets a little bigger. If it doesn't, axol learns that too. That's the same
reason a great record-store clerk is better than a playlist: they mostly give you what you want and
occasionally hand you something you didn't know you wanted.

**Why it can't easily be copied.** Three reasons.
1. The big platforms can't do this without hurting their own business. Their feeds exist to keep you
   inside their walls. axol's only job is your satisfaction.
2. Every day someone uses axol, their taste profile gets more precise, and that profile only exists in
   axol. Nobody else has a picture of what one person actually watches across three platforms.
3. When the next video platform appears, axol just adds it as another source. We own the layer that
   understands the person, not the videos.

**Where it stands.** The product works today: accounts, the plain-language signup, pulling videos from
all three platforms, the learning engine, the profile screen with controls, and a feed that feels
fast and smooth. It runs on a small, cheap server, and the AI costs a few cents per user per day.

**What's honest to say.** TikTok and Instagram don't give outside apps a search box. Automatic
discovery on those two needs a licensed data partner, which is a business deal, not a technical
problem. YouTube Shorts is fully automatic today. In the meantime users can share any TikTok or Reel
into axol and it's treated like any other video.

**What we're raising for.** Licensed access to TikTok and Instagram discovery, a proper phone app, and
a pilot with five thousand users so we can show two numbers: how much more of each video people
watch over their first fifty clips, and how many come back a week later.

**The one-sentence version.** axol is the first short-video feed that works for the viewer instead of
the platform, and it proves it by getting measurably better every time you watch.
